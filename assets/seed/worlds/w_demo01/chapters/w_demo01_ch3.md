# Chapter 3 — What the Ash Kept

"You're still reckless," said Mira, shoving through the smoke to grab his sleeve. "Seventeen years old and you'll get yourself killed before you find anything."

Kaelen let his sister pull him into the alley behind the smithy. His blue eyes stung from the smoke, and he blinked the tears away. She was fourteen and furious, and she had his memory-book clutched to her chest as always, the record of everything he kept forgetting.

"I had to," he said. "There wasn't time."

"There's always time to *think*." She flipped the book open. "You forgot something big just now, didn't you? I can see it on your face."

Before he could answer, a figure stepped out of the ruined archway at the alley's end — tall, cloaked, moving with a soldier's certainty. The firelight caught a face Kaelen knew from a hundred records and a thousand nightmares.

"Seraphine," he breathed.

The Ashen General inclined her head. "Hello, Kaelen. You've grown."

He could not speak. Seraphine Dain had died at the Siege of Velenhold, burned under the North Gate three winters ago. He had read the accounts. He had grieved her. And here she stood, alive, her boots crunching on the ash, her voice exactly as the records described it.

"That's not possible," Mira whispered. "You're dead. Everyone knows you're dead."

Seraphine smiled. "The ash keeps more than memories, child."
