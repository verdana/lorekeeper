# Chapter 1 — The Archivist's Boy

The flame remembered the fishmonger's stall, and Kaelen remembered the flame.

He crouched in the dark of the Deep Library's lowest vault, one candle guttering before him, and let his mind sink into it. Nineteen years old and already the courts whispered his name — the archivist's boy who could read a fire down to its last ember. The pale grey of his eyes caught the light and held it, ashen, unsettling, the way people always said.

On the back of his left hand, the Embermark glowed a dull orange. It had woken when he was seven, the night the fever took his mother, and it had never faded since. He could not remember her face now. He had bound too many flames since then, and each binding took something. That was the second law: *every binding costs a memory.* He had paid and paid.

It was well past midnight. Good. An Emberwright was nothing under the sun — the first law was iron — and so his work, like all his work, belonged to the night.

Somewhere above, the bells of Velenhold rang the third hour. He did not stop. He was looking, as he always looked, for the truth of the Siege three winters gone: who had broken the Covenant, who had killed, who had let the North Gate fall.

The candle burned lower. He borrowed nothing from it yet — a wright cannot make fire, only borrow it, and he was not ready to pay. He only watched, and let it remember for him.
